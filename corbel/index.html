Corbel

From the first stated need to the keys in hand.

Military construction, walked in order, with every requirement traced.

A suite for planning, programming, and delivering military construction. Each tool owns one stage of the work, shares one project record with the others, and shows the source behind every recommendation it makes.

From posture to close-out, each phase closed by a gate

Two layers. The theater tools – Adam, Paula, Earl, Piper and Vera – work across the portfolio: posture, priorities, decisions and assessment. The project tools follow each project through its phases and feed them.

These tools run in your browser. Use them with unclassified, publicly releasable information only. Controlled unclassified information belongs on government systems and AI services approved for it. The tools recommend; contracting officers and design agents decide.

Theater tools

In the order they are used: the posture the force needs, the portfolio that delivers it, whether each approach is right, whether each 1391 is sound, and whether execution is delivering.

Adam
Alignment, Deterrence, and Mission Advisor
Ready to use

Starts from the posture the force needs. ADAM records each capability required at a place by a date, links the projects that deliver it, and shows which project sets the date and how far it runs late. The mission score it works out flows straight into Paula’s prioritization.

Posture – ADAM
Paula
Portfolio Analytics, Updates, and Leadership Advisor
Ready to use

Sees the whole program at once. PAULA tracks cost, schedule, risk, and funds across every project, flags what needs leadership attention, and produces the status reports the chain of command asks for.

Portfolio – PAULA
Earl
Economic Analysis and Recommendation Ledger
Ready to use

Weighs every reasonable way to meet the need, from changing nothing to building new. EARL screens the alternatives, computes life-cycle cost and net present value, tests how sensitive the answer is, and writes the recommendation the project’s justification rests on.

Alternatives – EARL
Piper
Programming, Investment, and Project Estimate Reviewer
Ready to use

Creates, reviews, updates, and defends the DD Form 1391. PIPER builds the parametric estimate from the DoD Facilities Pricing Guide, red-teams the justification before headquarters does, keeps every version across budget cycles, and drafts answers to the questions reviewers ask.

1391 review – PIPER
Vera
Verification, Evaluation, and Risk Assessment
Ready to use

Assesses execution across a multi-site program: what is right and what is wrong, site by site, each finding traced to its evidence, rated and given a recommendation. It reads the portfolio, posture, 1391 and alternatives records, adds site visits and a schedule health check, and writes the assessment report and briefing.

Program assessment – VERA

VERA’s findings feed back into PAULA and ADAM.

Project delivery tools (7) – requirements, criteria, scope, SOW, RFIs, inspections, close-out
Nora
Needs, Options, and Requirements Analyst
Ready to use

Turns “we have a problem” into requirements that survive review. NORA identifies the stakeholders, runs the interviews and workshops, captures each requirement with the person who stated it, and measures the gap between what exists and what the mission needs. It seeds the traceability matrix every other tool builds on.

Requirement – NORA
Cora
Criteria Organizer and Regulatory Advisor
Ready to use

Determines which UFC, UFGS sections, FAR and DFARS clauses, and EM 385-1-1 and OSHA requirements apply to a project, with the reason and source for each, and a record of everything it left out. Exports a criteria list ready for the scope of work.

Criteria – CORA
Scott
Scope Content Organizer and Trigger Tracer
Ready to use

Reads a scope of work and breaks it into work elements and triggers: roof replacement, fall hazard, hot work, asbestos, CO2 fire suppression. Every finding quotes the line it came from. Runs entirely in your browser, or with two AI readers and an arbiter of your choosing.

Scope triggers – SCOTT
Sophie
Scope Of work Preparation, Harmonization, Integration, and Editing
Ready to use

Writes the scope of work in your format. One model drafts, a second reviews, a third settles their disagreements, and every “shall” traces back to a requirement. The finished scope runs back through SCOTT and CORA, so gaps surface before the solicitation does.

Statement of work – SOPHIE
Irma
Information Request Management Assistant
Ready to use

Answers requests for information and reviews submittals during construction. IRMA breaks each request into verifiable elements, grounds each one in the contract documents you load, and drafts advisory responses that cite their source.

RFIs and submittals – IRMA
Quinn
Quality, Unified Inspections, and Nonconformance Navigator
Ready to use

Keeps construction quality on the record. QUINN consolidates inspection reports, tracks deficiencies to closure, manages punch lists, and tells you when a facility is ready for occupancy.

Inspections – QUINN
Hank
Handover, Acceptance, and Notification Keeper
Ready to use

Closes the project out. HANK tracks turnover from substantial completion to final acceptance, prepares the DD Form 1354 data block by block, runs the warranty clock, works the close-out checklist with an owner and a source for every item, and files lessons learned where the next project will find them.

Close-out – HANK

Built by a program manager who has carried these documents through review, for the people who will carry them next.
